Cups in tarot: Ace to Five meanings

The Ace to Five of Cups follow feelings from an opening of the heart to loss and reappraisal. Each card has an upright and a reversed meaning.

By readmyspread7 November 2026

Cups are the suit of water in tarot, linked with feelings, relationships and intuition. The first five cards, Ace to Five, move from an opening of the heart through connection and celebration, and then into the quieter territory of boredom and loss. This guide uses the Rider-Waite-Smith (RWS) deck as its reference, and other decks may picture the cards differently.

Ace of Cups

New feeling, openness, emotional beginnings. A hand reaches from a cloud holding a cup that overflows with water, with a dove above it. It often speaks of love, compassion or a creative wellspring starting to flow. Reversed, it can suggest feelings held back, emotional exhaustion, or an offer you are not ready to accept.

Two of Cups

Partnership, mutual respect, connection. Two figures exchange cups, and above them hovers a winged lion's head over a staff. It is traditionally read as a meeting of equals, whether romantic, friendly or professional. Reversed, it may point to imbalance, miscommunication or a bond that feels one-sided.

Three of Cups

Friendship, celebration, community. Three figures raise their cups together amid harvest fruit. The card is about shared joy and support. Reversed, it can suggest gossip, overindulgence, or feeling left out of a group.

Four of Cups

Apathy, contemplation, reappraisal. A figure sits under a tree with arms crossed, three cups in front of them while a hand from a cloud offers a fourth. Many readers see it as a pause: boredom, or a withdrawal from what is on offer. It can ask what you are overlooking. Reversed, it may suggest re-engaging, noticing an opportunity, or coming out of a low period.

Five of Cups

Grief, regret, focusing on loss. A cloaked figure looks down at three spilled cups, while two stand upright behind them. A river and a bridge lie in the distance. The card is about attention: what has been lost is vivid, while what remains is easy to miss. Reversed, it can point to acceptance, forgiveness, or slowly turning around to look at what is left.

Reading the run as a story

Ace to Five of Cups can be read as a small emotional arc. A feeling opens, two people connect, a community celebrates, and then comes a stretch of dissatisfaction and loss. Neither of the last two cards is a prediction of hardship. They are invitations to notice how you are feeling, and Cups readings often become more useful once you name the feeling honestly.

In a spread

A Five of Cups in a position such as "what is in the way" might prompt you to ask what you are grieving or replaying. A Three of Cups in "what helps" could simply remind you to reach out to people. In all cases, your own response to the image is the best guide.

Keeping your own notes

Cups cards often stir up personal associations. Write down your first reaction to each image before checking a meaning. That first reaction, such as calm, unease or longing, is often the most relevant part of the reading.
