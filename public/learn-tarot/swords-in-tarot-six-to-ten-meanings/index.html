Swords in tarot: Six to Ten meanings

The Six to Ten of Swords carry the suit of thought through transition, secrecy, feeling trapped, worry and the end of a hard stretch.

By readmyspread11 November 2026

The second half of the Swords suit follows the thread of thought and communication through its harder stretches. In tarot, Swords are the suit of air, and the Six to Ten of Swords move from leaving trouble behind to reaching the bottom of it. Read as prompts for reflection, they are often more useful than their grim reputation suggests. This guide uses the Rider-Waite-Smith (RWS) deck as its reference, and other decks differ in the details.

Six of Swords

Transition, moving on, leaving rough waters. In RWS, a ferryman poles a boat carrying a covered figure and a child across water, with six swords standing upright in the boat. It suggests a passage from a troubled place to a calmer one, taken steadily rather than quickly. Reversed, it can point to being stuck, resisting a change, or carrying old worries into the new place.

Seven of Swords

Strategy, stealth, acting alone. A figure tiptoes away from a camp carrying five swords and leaving two behind. Many readers see cunning or evasion, while others see a careful, independent plan. Ask whether something is being avoided, left unsaid, or handled without telling others. Reversed, it often suggests coming clean, a guilty conscience or a plan that is being rethought.

Eight of Swords

Feeling trapped, limiting beliefs, restriction. A blindfolded, loosely bound figure stands among eight swords. Look closely and the bindings are loose and the way out is not blocked by the swords. The card is often read as a trap built from thinking, such as "I can't" or "I have no choice". Reversed, it suggests loosening the binds, seeing options again or asking for help.

Nine of Swords

Anxiety, worry, sleepless thoughts. A person sits up in bed with their face in their hands, nine swords on the wall behind. It names the kind of worry that grows at night. The card does not say the fear is true. It points to how heavy the thinking has become. Reversed, it can mean relief is coming, or that worry is being kept private and would ease if shared. If worry is overwhelming, speaking with a health professional or someone you trust is worth more than any card.

Ten of Swords

Painful ending, rock bottom, release. A figure lies face down with ten swords in their back, while a calm dawn breaks on the horizon. It looks dramatic, but it does not mean literal harm. It is read as an ending that has fully played out, after which there is nowhere further to fall. Reversed, it points to recovery, refusing to keep reliving the hurt, or an ending that drags on because it has not been accepted.

In a spread

These five cards often turn up when someone is thinking in circles. Note the position. In a "what is in the way" spot, the Eight can name a belief. In an "advice" spot, the Six can suggest putting distance between yourself and the problem. Pair them with a card from another suit for balance, such as a Cups card that speaks to feeling or a Pentacles card that grounds the next step.

A note on tone: this is the suit people tend to fear, yet it is also the one that names things honestly. Naming a worry or a pattern is often the first step to loosening it.
