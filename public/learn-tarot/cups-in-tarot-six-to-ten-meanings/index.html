Cups in tarot: Six to Ten meanings

The Six to Ten of Cups move from memory and daydreams to contentment and belonging. Each card has an upright and a reversed meaning.

By readmyspread8 November 2026

The second half of the suit of Cups moves from memory and choice towards contentment. Six to Ten of Cups, the water cards of tarot, cover nostalgia, imagination, moving on, wishes and the sense of a full emotional life. The images below follow the Rider-Waite-Smith (RWS) deck, and other decks may differ.

Six of Cups

Nostalgia, innocence, kindness. Two children in a courtyard share cups filled with flowers. It is traditionally a card of memory, childhood and simple generosity, and it can prompt you to ask what the past is offering you now. Reversed, it may suggest being stuck in the past, idealising how things were, or needing to grow beyond old patterns.

Seven of Cups

Options, imagination, daydreams. A silhouetted figure faces a cloud holding seven cups, each containing something different, such as a castle, jewels or a dragon. Many readers see a spread of possibilities that cannot all be real. It can ask which choices are worth pursuing. Reversed, it can suggest clarity arriving, a decision made, or the opposite: being swamped by choice.

Eight of Cups

Walking away, seeking more, letting go. A figure with a staff leaves eight stacked cups behind, heading into hills under a moon. The cups are neatly arranged but one gap shows something is missing. It is often about leaving something that no longer fulfils you. Reversed, it may point to staying out of fear, drifting, or returning to something unresolved.

Nine of Cups

Contentment, satisfaction, wishes. A man sits with arms folded in front of a curved shelf holding nine cups. It is sometimes called the wish card, and it often speaks of comfort and enjoying what you have earned. Reversed, it can suggest smugness, hollow pleasure, or a wish that looks different once it is granted.

Ten of Cups

Harmony, belonging, emotional fulfilment. A couple with two children stand beneath a rainbow of ten cups, a home in the distance. It is traditionally the picture of a happy household, though many readers take it more broadly to mean feeling at home in your relationships and values. Reversed, it may point to family tension, a gap between the ideal and the real, or a need to redefine what fulfilment means to you.

Reading the run as a story

Looking back at the five together, you can trace a loop: memory, fantasy, departure, satisfaction, and belonging. The Eight is the turning point where dissatisfaction becomes action. It is a useful reminder that a Cups arc does not move in a straight line, and any card can sit anywhere in your reading.

In a spread

An Eight of Cups in a position such as "what to let go" might invite you to ask what you have outgrown, while a Seven in "what you can do" could prompt you to narrow a long list of options to one or two.

Keeping your own notes

Several of these cards, especially the Six and the Ten, tend to bring up memories. Jot down what comes to mind, since personal associations are part of how a card speaks to you. Keep your notes descriptive and avoid treating them as predictions.
