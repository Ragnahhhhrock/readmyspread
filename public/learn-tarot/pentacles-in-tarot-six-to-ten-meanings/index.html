Pentacles in tarot: Six to Ten meanings

The Six to Ten of Pentacles cover giving, patience, skill, comfort and legacy, the steadier and more settled half of the earth suit.

By readmyspread14 November 2026

The second half of the Pentacles suit moves from sharing resources to lasting stability. In tarot, Pentacles stand for earth: work, home, the body and what we build over time. The Six to Ten of Pentacles are read as prompts for reflection on giving, patience, effort, self-reliance and legacy. This guide uses the Rider-Waite-Smith (RWS) deck as its reference, and decks vary in the details.

Six of Pentacles

Generosity, giving and receiving, fairness. A well-dressed figure holds scales and gives coins to two kneeling people. The card raises questions of who gives, who receives and whether the exchange is fair. It can be warm or it can carry strings. Reversed, it may point to debts, uneven exchange or generosity with conditions attached.

Seven of Pentacles

Patience, assessment, long-term effort. A gardener leans on a hoe and looks at a bush heavy with pentacles. It is a pause to check the work so far. Is it growing as hoped, and is it worth the effort? Reversed, it can suggest impatience, frustration at slow progress or effort put into the wrong thing.

Eight of Pentacles

Diligence, craftsmanship, learning a skill. A craftsperson sits at a bench, carving pentacles one by one, with finished ones on display. It is steady, focused practice. Reversed, it may mean repetitive work with no meaning, perfectionism or cutting corners.

Nine of Pentacles

Self-sufficiency, comfort, enjoying what you have built. A richly dressed figure stands in a vineyard, with a bird on one hand. It is often read as independence earned through effort, along with the pleasure of a calm and tended space. Reversed, it can suggest over-reliance on appearances, feeling that comfort is out of reach or difficulty relaxing even when things are fine.

Ten of Pentacles

Legacy, belonging, long-term stability. An older man sits beneath an archway with a family and dogs, a town behind them. It speaks of what is passed down and what lasts: family, tradition and foundations built over years. Reversed, it can point to family tension, shaky foundations or the weight of inherited expectations.

In a spread

Reading this run in order is tempting, but a spread cares about position. The Seven of Pentacles in a "what helps" position, for instance, may simply invite patience. The Eight can say that practice matters more than talent. Ask where in your life you are tending, sharing or resting, and what the card suggests about balancing those. None of it is a forecast of income, assets or family events.

Several of these cards deal with time. The Seven waits, the Eight practises, the Nine enjoys and the Ten inherits. If one of them lands in your spread, ask what pace you are working at, and whether it suits you.

Tarot has no demonstrated ability to predict events. Readings are for entertainment and reflection only, and they aren't medical, legal or financial advice.
