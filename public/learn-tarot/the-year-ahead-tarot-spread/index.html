The year-ahead tarot spread

The year-ahead tarot spread uses a theme card plus one card for each month. It works best as a prompt for planning and reflection, not as a forecast.

By readmyspread26 October 2026

The year-ahead tarot spread uses thirteen cards: one for the overall theme of the year, then one for each month. It is a popular choice around birthdays and the new year, and it works best as a planning and reflection tool rather than a forecast. Tarot cannot tell you what will happen in March or September, but a card can prompt you to think about what you want each month to hold.

How the layout works

Place the first card at the top centre. This is your theme card, a lens for the whole year. Below it, lay twelve cards in two rows of six. January to June go on the upper row, left to right, and July to December on the lower row. You can start from the month you are in rather than January if that suits you better, as long as you label the order before you turn anything over.

Some readers lay the months in a circle instead. The two-row version is easier to photograph and to read at a glance, which is why it is shown here.

Preparing the question

A broad question is fine here, because the spread already provides structure. Try something like "What might I focus on this year?" or "What themes could shape my year?" Avoid questions that ask for events, such as "Will I get a promotion in June?" The cards are better suited to asking what you could pay attention to, or what a month could ask of you.

Reading the theme card first

Turn over the theme card and sit with it before reading any months. Write down a few words about what you see. Then ask how this card might colour the rest of the spread. If the theme card is the Three of Wands, for example, you might hold the idea of looking ahead and planning in mind as you read the months. If it is the Hermit, the year may call for time alone and careful thought. These are prompts, and you decide what rings true.

Reading each month

Read the months in order, and note how the cards relate to each other. A few things to look for:

  • Patterns of suit. Several Cups in a row might suggest a stretch where feelings or relationships come to the front. Several Pentacles may point to work and practical matters.
  • Major arcana. A major card in a month can mark something you may want to give more attention to.
  • Shifts in tone. A heavy card followed by a lighter one can show a change of pace in how you approach things.
  • Gaps and repeats. If the same number or card turns up twice, ask what that echo could mean.

Keep each reading short. A couple of sentences per month is plenty, and trying to squeeze every detail from twelve cards at once usually backfires.

Making it useful

Write the reading in a notebook with the date. Then, instead of waiting to see whether things come true, check in at the start of each month. Look at the card for that month and ask what you would like from it. Then, at the end of the month, add a line about what actually happened. This turns the spread into a record of your own reflection, and it protects you from reading meaning into events afterward.

If a month's card feels unwelcome, you do not have to take it as a warning. Ask what it would mean to prepare for that kind of energy, or what support you would want around you.

Tarot has no demonstrated ability to predict events. Readings are for entertainment and reflection only, and they aren't medical, legal or financial advice.
