The Wheel of Fortune to Temperance: tarot card meanings

Major arcana X to XIV cover turning points, fairness, pause, endings and balance. Here is what each tarot card traditionally means, upright and reversed.

By readmyspread25 October 2026

The middle stretch of the major arcana, from the Wheel of Fortune (X) to Temperance (XIV), moves through turning points, fairness, pause, endings and recovery. These five tarot cards are about how we respond to change, which is why they tend to come up when a reading touches on a transition. Meanings below follow the Rider-Waite-Smith (RWS) deck, and other decks differ in details.

Each card has an upright meaning and a short note on the reversed meaning. Reversals are optional, and you can ignore them if you prefer. Treat every meaning as a prompt for reflection rather than a prediction.

The Wheel of Fortune (X)

The RWS card shows a large wheel in the sky, with a sphinx on top and four winged figures in the corners. Cycles, change, turning points, chance. Upright, it points to the way circumstances rise and fall, and invites you to ask which parts of a situation you can influence and which you can only meet with flexibility.

Reversed: resisting a change, feeling stuck in a repeating pattern, or a sense of things going against you. It can ask whether a habit is keeping a loop going.

Justice (XI)

A seated figure holds scales in one hand and a raised sword in the other. Fairness, truth, cause and effect, accountability. It often prompts questions about honesty, weighing evidence, and taking responsibility for your choices. In a spread, it can ask what a fair outcome would look like for everyone involved, including you.

Some decks number Justice VIII and Strength XI. The RWS deck uses Strength VIII and Justice XI, which is the order used here.

Reversed: unfairness, dishonesty, avoiding responsibility, or a one-sided view of events.

The Hanged Man (XII)

A figure hangs upside down from a wooden frame by one foot, calm, with a glow around the head. Pause, surrender, new perspective, letting go of control. This card is less about punishment than about stepping back so you can see differently. It can suggest that waiting or looking from another angle is more useful than pushing on.

Reversed: stalling, needless sacrifice, or resisting a pause that would help. It can ask whether you are waiting out of wisdom or out of fear.

Death (XIII)

A skeleton in armour rides a white horse. Endings, transformation, transition, clearing space. In tarot, Death is almost never about literal death. It speaks of something finishing so that something else can begin: a role, a habit, a chapter. It can be a hard card to sit with, and it can also be a relief.

Reversed: holding on to what has run its course, fear of change, or a slow transition that is not yet complete.

Temperance (XIV)

An angel pours liquid between two cups, with one foot on land and one in water. Balance, moderation, patience, blending. It suggests finding a measured middle way, mixing different needs or ideas, and letting things develop at their own pace. It often follows Death in the sequence, a gentle recovery after an ending.

Reversed: imbalance, excess, impatience, or pulling in two directions at once.

Reading these cards together

Placed in order, the five read like a short story about change: a turn (Wheel), an honest reckoning (Justice), a pause to see differently (Hanged Man), an ending (Death) and a gradual rebalancing (Temperance). You do not need to see them as a sequence in a spread, but the order can help you remember them.

In a spread, notice the position as well as the card. Death in a "what to let go" position reads differently from Death in a "challenge" position. Ask what the card shows, then how it relates to your question.
