The court cards of Swords describe four ways of using the mind: curiosity, speed, clarity and authority. In tarot, Swords belong to air, the element of thought and communication. Court cards can stand for other people in your life, for parts of yourself, or for an approach you might take. Treat them as a mirror and not as a verdict. This guide uses the Rider-Waite-Smith (RWS) deck as its reference, and some decks rename the courts, for example Princess, Prince, Queen and King.

Page of Swords

Curiosity, new ideas, a keen eye. The Page stands alert, sword raised, in a windy landscape. It is the student of the suit: asking questions, gathering information, eager to speak up. Reversed, it can suggest gossip, careless words or ideas that are all talk and no follow-through.

Knight of Swords

Drive, speed, directness. The Knight is usually shown charging forward with sword held high. He stands for acting on an idea at once, with determination and little pause. As an approach, it is bold and focused. Reversed, it can mean rushing, bluntness that hurts, or scattered energy. It is worth asking whether a little patience would help.

Queen of Swords

Clarity, honesty, independence. The Queen sits with her sword upright, looking steadily ahead. She is often read as someone who speaks plainly, sets clear boundaries and values truth over comfort. Reversed, the same clarity can turn cold or critical, or can be held back out of guardedness.

King of Swords

Authority, logic, fair judgement. The King holds his sword upright from his throne. He represents reasoned decisions, expertise and communication that is both precise and fair. Reversed, he can suggest rigidity, using knowledge to dominate, or a decision that ignores feeling altogether.

Reading the court

When a Swords court card appears, a few questions help:

  • Does it sound like a real person? If so, what do they bring to the question, and what might you be projecting?
  • Does it describe a quality you could use, such as the Page's questions or the Queen's honesty?
  • Is it showing a habit you lean on too much, such as the Knight's haste or the King's detachment?

The position helps decide. In a spot for "you", it speaks about approach. In a spot for "others", it may describe a person, or the way their input lands. There is rarely only one right answer, and it is fine to hold two readings side by side.

How they differ from the numbered Swords

The numbered cards tend to describe situations and the court cards describe the people, or the stance, within them. A Six of Swords beside a Queen of Swords, for instance, might read as a move toward calm handled with clear-eyed honesty.

Because Swords deal in words, a court card here often points to how something is said as much as what is said. Notice whether a conversation needs the Page's questions, the Knight's directness, the Queen's honesty or the King's fairness.

Tarot has no demonstrated ability to predict events. Readings are for entertainment and reflection only, and they aren't medical, legal or financial advice.
