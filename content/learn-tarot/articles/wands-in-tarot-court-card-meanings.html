The court cards of Wands are the Page, Knight, Queen and King. In tarot, court cards can be read as other people in your life, as parts of yourself, or as approaches you might take. Wands court cards carry the energy of fire: enthusiasm, confidence and the drive to act. This guide uses the Rider-Waite-Smith (RWS) deck as its reference, and decks vary.

Page of Wands

Curiosity, enthusiasm, new ideas. The Page stands in a desert landscape and looks closely at the wand held in one hand. It can describe a keen beginner, a message about a new project, or a part of you that wants to explore. Reversed, it may point to scattered interests, impatience, or an idea that has not been tested.

Knight of Wands

Passion, speed, adventure. The Knight rides a rearing horse, armour covered with a pattern, wand held high. This is the most energetic of the four, often read as someone acting boldly and quickly. Reversed, it can suggest recklessness, burnout, or enthusiasm that starts more than it finishes.

Queen of Wands

Warmth, confidence, magnetism. The Queen sits on her throne with a sunflower in one hand and a wand in the other, a black cat at her feet. She is traditionally read as a person at ease with who they are and generous with their energy. Reversed, the card may suggest jealousy, insecurity, or a need for attention outweighing real connection.

King of Wands

Vision, leadership, decisiveness. The King sits on a throne decorated with lions and salamanders, holding a flowering wand. He represents someone who turns inspiration into plans and takes responsibility for them. Reversed, it can mean overbearing behaviour, impulsiveness, or a leader who has lost sight of the people involved.

Reading court cards

A few ways to approach them in a spread:

  • As a person. Ask whether someone in your life matches the description. Treat it as a way to notice qualities, not as a verdict on a real individual.
  • As part of you. Ask which quality the card is highlighting in yourself right now.
  • As an approach. Ask what it would look like to act in this way, with a Knight's speed or a Queen's warmth.

Rank also gives clues. Pages tend to be about learning and messages, Knights about movement, Queens about inner mastery, and Kings about outer authority. Some decks rename the ranks, for instance Princess, Prince, Queen and King, or Knave, Knight, Queen and King, but the pattern stays similar.

A quick example

If the Knight of Wands appears in a position like "your approach", you might ask whether your pace suits the situation. If the Queen appears as "what helps", the prompt may be to lean on confidence and warmth rather than force.

Telling the court apart

If the four feel similar at first, compare them by how they use energy. The Page experiments, the Knight charges ahead, the Queen draws people in, and the King directs the whole effort. Asking which of those four verbs fits your situation can be a quick way into the card.

Tarot has no demonstrated ability to predict events. Readings are for entertainment and reflection only, and they aren't medical, legal or financial advice.
