The Sun (XIX), Judgement (XX) and the World (XXI) are the last three cards of the major arcana. After the uncertainty of the Moon, they move toward clarity, a sense of calling and completion. Many readers see them as the closing stretch of the Fool's journey, though they can turn up in any reading. Meanings below follow the Rider-Waite-Smith (RWS) deck, and each tarot card is offered as a prompt for reflection.

The Sun (XIX)

A child rides a white horse beneath a bright sun, with sunflowers behind a wall. Joy, vitality, clarity, optimism. The Sun is one of the most straightforward cards in the deck. It points to warmth, openness and things being seen plainly. In a spread, it can ask what is going well, or where you might allow yourself to feel pleased.

Reversed: a dimmed mood, temporary clouds, or optimism that has not been tested. It can also suggest being too hard on yourself when things are mostly fine.

Judgement (XX)

An angel sounds a trumpet while figures rise from their coffins with arms open. Reflection, reckoning, calling, forgiveness, renewal. The name can sound harsh, but the card is mostly about taking an honest look at your path and responding to an inner call. It often arrives when someone is reassessing, forgiving themselves or another person, or ready to make a fresh start.

Reversed: self-doubt, harsh self-criticism, or avoiding a decision you already sense. It can ask what you are not letting yourself hear.

The World (XXI)

A figure dances inside a large wreath, with four creatures in the corners. Completion, integration, wholeness, accomplishment. The World marks the end of a cycle and the sense of arriving. Because the end of one cycle leads to the next, many readers also see it as a point of looking back before beginning again. It can prompt you to acknowledge what you have achieved.

Reversed: loose ends, a nearly finished project, or reluctance to close a chapter. It can ask what is still needed to feel complete.

How the three connect

Read in order, the three form a simple arc: the Sun shows things in clear light, Judgement asks what you will do with that clarity, and the World marks a cycle finished. In a spread you do not need to see this arc, but it can help you remember the cards and notice when they speak to one another. If two of the three appear together, ask which part of that arc your question is in.

Reading them in a spread

  • Resist reading them as guarantees. A bright card in an outcome position is a prompt to consider what a good result would look like, not a promise.
  • Use position. The World as "what to release" asks something quite different from the World as "outcome".
  • Notice your response. If a lovely card feels hollow, or a challenging one feels fine, that reaction is part of the reading.

Decks differ in how they depict these cards, though the core ideas are similar across most.

Tarot has no demonstrated ability to predict events. Readings are for entertainment and reflection only, and they aren't medical, legal or financial advice.
