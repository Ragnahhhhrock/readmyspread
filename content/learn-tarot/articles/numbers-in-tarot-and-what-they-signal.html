Every suit in a tarot deck runs from Ace to Ten, and the number carries a theme of its own. Once you know what each number tends to signal, you can make a reasonable first guess at a minor arcana card before you recall its specific title. This guide covers the ten numbers and how they combine with the suits. The meanings here are conventions many readers share, and decks and readers vary.

The ten numbers

  • Ace: beginnings. A seed, an offer, a spark in its purest form.
  • Two: balance and choice. Two things in relation, a decision or a partnership.
  • Three: growth and teamwork. The first result, something shared or expanding.
  • Four: stability and pause. A foundation, a rest, sometimes a rut.
  • Five: conflict and change. Disruption, competition, a test of the foundation.
  • Six: harmony and movement. Recovery, a way through, a more settled rhythm.
  • Seven: reflection and challenge. A pause to assess, or a test of resolve.
  • Eight: skill and momentum. Focus, effort, speed or being stuck in motion.
  • Nine: resilience and near completion. Almost there, with strain or reward.
  • Ten: completion and overload. A cycle ends, either fulfilled or too heavy to carry.

Reading number and suit together

The number is the plot and the suit is the setting. A Five is always a point of friction, but a Five of Cups is friction around feeling, the Five of Swords is friction in words and thought, and the Five of Pentacles is friction in work or resources. The four suits give the arena: Wands for energy and action, Cups for feelings and relationships, Swords for thought and communication, and Pentacles for work and the practical world.

This makes an unfamiliar card more approachable. A Seven of Wands can be read as a challenge (Seven) around action or drive (Wands) before any memorised meaning is brought in.

A simple arc

Many readers see the numbers as a small journey. One to three is starting and building, four to six is settling and meeting resistance, seven to nine is testing and refining, and ten is the close of the cycle that makes room for the next Ace. It is not a rule, but it explains why the Ten is so often both an ending and a relief.

Counting numbers in a spread

Some readers notice patterns across a whole layout. Several Fours might suggest a lot of pausing and consolidating, and a handful of Fives suggests change in the air. Several Aces point to new starts. Treat such patterns as a lens and a conversation starter, not a verdict. The major arcana have their own numbers, but those follow a separate sequence and are usually read through the card itself.

The numbers also help when memory fails. If you cannot recall the Eight of Cups, you know Eight suggests momentum and Cups concern feeling, which is enough to start a sensible reading and check the details afterward.

Tarot has no demonstrated ability to predict events. Readings are for entertainment and reflection only, and they aren't medical, legal or financial advice.
