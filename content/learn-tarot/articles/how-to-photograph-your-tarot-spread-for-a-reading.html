A clear photo makes the difference between a tarot spread that can be read accurately and one that cannot. Every card has to be identifiable, and the position of each card matters as much as the card itself. This guide covers lighting, angle, spacing and framing, and a few quick checks before you press the button. Nothing here needs special equipment: a phone camera and a table are enough.

Light

Even light is the aim. Daylight from a window works well, as long as it falls across the table rather than directly onto the cards. Harsh sunlight and overhead lamps can produce glare on glossy cards, which hides faces and suit symbols. Shadows from your hand or phone can hide detail too, so check the edges before you shoot. If a card shines, tilt the lamp or move a little to one side of the window.

Angle

Shoot from directly above, with your phone held level, parallel to the table. A tilted angle makes the nearest cards look larger and the far ones squashed, and it can make the order of positions harder to follow. It helps to stand and hold the phone over the middle of the spread. Keep your arms and shadow out of the frame.

Spacing and framing

Leave a small gap between cards so each outline is clear. Overlapping is fine in some layouts, such as the Celtic Cross where one card is laid across another, but keep the covered card's name and number visible. Check the following before taking the picture:

  • The whole spread is in the frame, with a little room around every edge.
  • No card is cut off, partly covered or hidden behind an object.
  • Cards are laid in the order of the spread you are using.
  • Reversed cards are left reversed, so the reading can recognise them.

Background

A plain surface, such as a cloth, a table or a sheet of paper, keeps attention on the cards. Busy patterns, books, cups and jewellery add clutter and can confuse which items are cards. A dark cloth often suits light-edged cards and a light cloth suits dark-edged ones.

Before you send it

Look at the photo at full size. Can you read every card name or suit symbol? If you can zoom in and still read each one, the photo is good. If not, retake it. Straightening the photo, rather than adding filters, keeps colours true. Name the spread you used if you can, since the same cards mean different things in different positions.

If your table is small, work in sections only if you must, but a single photo of the full layout is far better. A step stool or a chair to stand on can give a higher, steadier vantage point for large spreads.
