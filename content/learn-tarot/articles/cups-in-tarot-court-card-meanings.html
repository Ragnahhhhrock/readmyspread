The court cards of Cups are the Page, Knight, Queen and King. They carry the qualities of water: sensitivity, imagination, empathy and the way we relate to others. As with all court cards in tarot, they can describe people in your life, aspects of yourself, or approaches you might try. This guide uses the Rider-Waite-Smith (RWS) deck as its reference, and decks differ.

Page of Cups

Curiosity, tenderness, creative messages. The Page stands by the sea holding a cup from which a small fish peeks out. It is a playful image, and many readers link it with a surprising idea, a gentle message or a part of you that is open and imaginative. Reversed, it may suggest emotional immaturity, creative block, or oversensitivity.

Knight of Cups

Romance, invitation, following the heart. The Knight rides a calm horse, holding out a cup. Unlike the Knight of Wands, this rider moves steadily rather than at speed. He is traditionally associated with offers, idealism and sincere feeling. Reversed, it can suggest moodiness, unrealistic expectations, or charm that does not match action.

Queen of Cups

Compassion, intuition, emotional depth. The Queen sits on a throne at the water's edge, holding an ornate, closed cup that she studies closely. She represents someone who listens, feels deeply and trusts intuition. Reversed, the card may point to being overwhelmed by others' feelings, withdrawing, or losing boundaries.

King of Cups

Emotional balance, diplomacy, care. The King sits on a throne that appears to float on the sea, a fish leaping nearby and a ship in the distance. He is commonly read as someone who holds strong feelings steady and responds with calm. Reversed, it can suggest suppressed emotion, manipulation, or composure used as a mask.

Reading court cards

Three questions can help when a court card appears:

  • Who does this resemble? A friend, colleague or family member might match, though the card is not a verdict on them.
  • Where does this live in me? Ask which quality you are leaning on, or avoiding.
  • What would this approach look like? For example, what would it mean to respond with a Queen's attentiveness?

In the suit of Cups, the ranks can be read as stages of emotional maturity. The Page is learning to feel, the Knight acts on feeling, the Queen understands feeling, and the King manages it. That idea is a convention, not a rule, and it works best as a loose guide.

In a spread

A Queen of Cups in the "you" position could invite you to ask how you are listening to yourself. A Knight of Cups in "what is coming" might prompt you to consider what you would like to offer or accept.

Telling the court apart

A quick comparison can help: the Page is curious, the Knight is sincere, the Queen is attuned and the King is steady. If you are unsure which court card is speaking in a reading, ask which of those four words best fits the feeling in your question.

Tarot has no demonstrated ability to predict events. Readings are for entertainment and reflection only, and they aren't medical, legal or financial advice.
