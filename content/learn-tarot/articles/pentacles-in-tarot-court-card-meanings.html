The court cards of Pentacles show four ways of dealing with the practical world: learning, persistence, nurturing and mastery. In tarot, Pentacles belong to earth, so these cards are about work, home, the body and the slow building of things. Court cards can be other people, aspects of yourself or approaches you might take. This guide uses the Rider-Waite-Smith (RWS) deck as its reference, and some decks use different court titles or call the suit Coins.

Page of Pentacles

Study, curiosity about the practical, a first step. The Page stands in a green field, gazing at a single pentacle held carefully. This is the beginner who wants to learn properly: a new skill, a new course, a plan to try. Reversed, it can suggest daydreaming without action, lost focus or hesitation to start.

Knight of Pentacles

Reliability, patience, steady progress. The Knight is usually shown on a still horse, holding a pentacle and looking at it closely. Of the four knights he is the slowest and most methodical. Reversed, the strength can turn into stubbornness, boredom with routine or being stuck in a rut.

Queen of Pentacles

Care, resourcefulness, a welcoming home. The Queen sits among flowers and greenery, cradling a pentacle in her lap. She is often read as the practical nurturer who makes a place feel comfortable and balances work with warmth. Reversed, she can suggest over-giving, neglecting your own needs or being weighed down by practical worries.

King of Pentacles

Stability, experience, generous leadership. The King sits among vines and abundance, hand on a pentacle. He stands for mastery earned over time and for providing for others. Reversed, he may point to rigidity, valuing status over people or being driven by a need for control.

Using the court in a reading

Court cards ask for a little interpretation. Three questions help:

  • Is this a person? Think about who in the situation shows these qualities, and be cautious about assuming too much.
  • Is it you? A Page can show where you are a learner, and a Queen where you are caring.
  • Is it an approach? The Knight's patience might be exactly what a stalled project needs.

The reversed versions are not "bad" people. They usually show a strength pushed too far or not yet used. A Knight of Pentacles reversed might simply be a reminder that steadiness has tipped into stuckness.

Comparing the ranks

The same rank often carries a similar temperament across suits. Pages learn, Knights pursue, Queens nurture, Kings steward. In Pentacles, each of these is expressed through work and daily life rather than through ideas, feelings or ambition.

One practical tip: if two Pentacles courts appear together, ask how they differ. A Page beside a King, for instance, might describe the gap between a beginner and an expert, or between where you are and where you want to be.

Tarot has no demonstrated ability to predict events. Readings are for entertainment and reflection only, and they aren't medical, legal or financial advice.
