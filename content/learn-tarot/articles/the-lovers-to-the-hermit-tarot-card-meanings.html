The next four cards of the major arcana, The Lovers (VI) to The Hermit (IX), move from choice to drive, then to gentle courage and finally to solitude. This guide gives the upright meaning of each, a note on reversals and a short tip for reading it in a spread. It uses the Rider-Waite-Smith (RWS) deck as the reference, so imagery described here may differ in other decks.

One point to know first: in RWS, Strength is numbered VIII and Justice is XI. Some older decks swap those two numbers, so check the numerals on your own cards.

The Lovers (VI)

Keywords: choice, values, union, alignment. In the RWS version, two figures stand beneath an angel. The card is often linked to romance, but many readers see it as more about choices that express who you are. It asks what you value and whether your actions match. In a relationship spread it can point to connection and honest communication.

Reversed: it may suggest a mismatch between values and choices, avoiding a decision or a strain in a relationship.

In a spread: look at which choice the card is putting in front of you, not only at who the lovers might be.

The Chariot (VII)

Keywords: drive, determination, direction, control. A figure stands in a chariot drawn by two sphinxes, one light and one dark, which many readers take to represent opposing forces being held together by will. The card is about moving forward with focus and bringing competing pulls into one direction.

Reversed: it can suggest losing direction, forcing things, or being pulled in two ways at once.

In a spread: ask what you are steering and whether you have chosen the direction yourself.

Strength (VIII)

Keywords: courage, patience, compassion, inner strength. The image shows a woman calmly holding the mouth of a lion. The strength on display is gentle, not forceful. The card often points to meeting a strong feeling or difficult situation with patience and care rather than pushing against it.

Reversed: it may suggest self-doubt, running low on energy or struggling to stay composed.

In a spread: in an advice position, it can invite you to try softness before force.

The Hermit (IX)

Keywords: solitude, reflection, guidance, inner search. A robed figure stands on a height, holding a lantern and staff. It suggests stepping back to look inward, and it can also stand for a mentor or someone who has walked the road already. Solitude here is chosen and purposeful.

Reversed: it may point to isolation, withdrawing too far or avoiding reflection.

In a spread: ask whether you need quiet to think, or whether you have been alone with the question too long.

Reading the four together

Read as a sequence, the group moves from a choice (Lovers) to committed action (Chariot), then to a quieter way of handling pressure (Strength) and finally to turning inward (Hermit). Treat that as one way to remember the order, not a rule. Each card also stands on its own, and each is a prompt for your own reflection.

Tarot has no demonstrated ability to predict events. Readings are for entertainment and reflection only, and they aren't medical, legal or financial advice.
